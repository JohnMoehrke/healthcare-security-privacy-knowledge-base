# EHR not used securely

Originally posted: https://healthcaresecprivacy.blogspot.com/2010/04/ehr-not-used-securely.html
Published: 2010-04-07T02:00:00.002Z
Updated: 2010-04-07T03:14:48.826Z
Author: John Moehrke

---

I ran across multiple articles this past week on the topic of EHR security. They were all shocked at the results, but I am not. The reason I am not surprised is that this is the first year when it really became important for a Healthcare Provider Organization to care about security. Sure in past years they should have been concerned with it, but we all know that organizations (or individuals) will not take security seriously until your neighbors are being publicly flogged. One hopes that it is their neighbor, but someone must be the bad guy. What changed is the Breach Notification, and specifically the HHS notification of breaches. I covered this just last week in the article [A  Look into the HHS Posts Data Breach Notifications.](http://healthcaresecprivacy.blogspot.com/2010/03/look-into-hhs-posts-data-breach.html)

This means that Provider Organizations are just now talking about it.  It will be slightly better next year, not because they are not changing, well some will be changing, but rather because it takes quite a bit of effort to make changes to a system that is not secure by design. (see #2 on my[Three  Security Concerns for 2010)](http://healthcaresecprivacy.blogspot.com/2010/02/three-security-concerns-for-2010.html). The pressure needs to be kept up month after month (I also note three new breach notifications in healthcare just last week).

This article acts surprised that Healthcare Organizations are reactive and not proactive... This is because they have not had to be proactive, and lacking a motivation they will not be proactive:

> **[Healthcare industry overlooks critical gaps in data security:](http://www.net-security.org/secworld.php?id=9102&utm_source=feedburner&utm_medium=feed&utm_campaign=Feed%3A+HelpNetSecurity+%28Help+Net+Security%29&utm_content=Google+Reader)**

The 2010 HIMSS Analytics Report: Security of Patient Data indicates that  healthcare organizations are actively taking steps to ensure that  patient data is secure. However, these efforts appear to be more  reactive than proactive, as hospitals dedicate more resources toward  breach response vs. breach prevention through risk management  activities. [More](http://www.net-security.org/secworld.php?id=9102&utm_source=feedburner&utm_medium=feed&utm_campaign=Feed%3A+HelpNetSecurity+%28Help+Net+Security%29&utm_content=Google+Reader)
This article boldly declares that EMR Data Theft is on the rise. Well of course it is, as data is moved into EMRs there are more EMRs to steal data from. This data could simply be telling us of the ever increasing use of  EMR.

> **[EMR Data Theft Booming](http://www.informationweek.com/news/healthcare/security-privacy/showArticle.jhtml?articleID=224200494&cid=nl_healthcare_2010-03-31_h)**

Fraud resulting from exposure of electronic medical records has risen from 3% in 2008 to 7% in 2009, a 112% increase, researcher says. [More](http://www.informationweek.com/news/healthcare/security-privacy/showArticle.jhtml?articleID=224200494&cid=nl_healthcare_2010-03-31_h)
And this one finds 'mixed results'. The story they weave is that Provider Organizations say they are compliant, but yet breaches are up. Not very useful.

> **[Survey Finds Mixed Results on Security of Electronic Health Data](http://www.ihealthbeat.org/articles/2010/4/6/survey-finds-mixed-results-on-security-of-electronic-health-data.aspx)**

> Health care professionals rated their organizations high for compliance  with health IT regulations, but reports of data breaches in the past  year were up from two years ago, according to a new biannual report released Monday, Health Data Management reports. [More](http://www.ihealthbeat.org/articles/2010/4/6/survey-finds-mixed-results-on-security-of-electronic-health-data.aspx)
It is true what they say: [Lies, damned lies, and  statistics.](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lies,_damned_lies,_and_statistics) Yes we must get better, but we will get better by using Risk Assessment to apply reasonable controls against real risks.
