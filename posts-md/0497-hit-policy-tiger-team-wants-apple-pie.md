# HIT Policy Tiger team wants Apple Pie

Published: 2010-11-01T03:19:00Z
Updated: 2010-11-01T03:19:28.152Z
Author: John Moehrke

---

If only they could provide the recipe, rather than simply ranting about what it should smell and look like. I am in no way saying that this recipe is an easy thing to produce. In [Stepping stones for Privacy Consent](http://healthcaresecprivacy.blogspot.com/2010/08/stepping-stones-for-privacy-consent.html), I am actually saying that we need to get going trying a bunch of recipes to see if any of them will work. There are Health Information Exchanges out there that are trying. I just don't think that there is much in the way of lessons to learn yet. This might take a while to determine what really works vs what sounds good in theory.

I like that the Tiger team is starting with the HHS white paper on [Consumer Consent Options](http://healthit.hhs.gov/portal/server.pt/gateway/PTARGS_0_11673_911197_0_0_18/ChoiceModelFinal032610.pdf). This paper is really well written, and does face some reality around priorities. The first instrument should be a blunt one: [Opt-In, and     Opt-Out. For special cases simply don't publish THAT!](http://healthcaresecprivacy.blogspot.com/2009/10/opt-in-opt-out-dont-publish-that.html) . Not a very elegant tool, but for those willing to take the risk, they get the reward of the benefits that a HIE offers. For those not willing, they get to stay out. Then encourage creativity.

[Accountability using Audit Controls is critical to success.](http://healthcaresecprivacy.blogspot.com/2010/05/accountability-using-atna-audit.html) We must know what happens with the information regardless of the consent rules.

I encourage anyone to let me know if they know of a HIE that has any form of consent, including simple OPT-IN or simple OPT-OUT. I would be very glad to share best practices.
