I am a longstanding customer of [23andMe](http://www.23andme.com/), and am very disappointed at the [current behavior](http://blog.23andme.com/news/23andme-provides-an-update-regarding-fdas-review/). 23andMe have decided to [stop offering a healthcare service](http://blog.23andme.com/news/23andme-provides-an-update-regarding-fdas-review/) rather than work with the FDA to achieve safe offering. This approach to questions of patient safety from the FDA seems to be very typical of new product groups. This is not unique to 32andMe. I am not upset at FDA, I am an advocate of [Safety](http://healthcaresecprivacy.blogspot.com/2013/04/safety-vs-privacy.html). I was introduced to the FDA mechanisms 16 years ago. mHealth innovation vs Safety
There is much concern that FDA will quash innovation in mHealth. If one only looks at the negative that FDA brings, that of process rigor and paperwork, I can understand the worry. BUT one must look at what this process rigor and paperwork brings relative to protecting patient safety. Safety really must be a high priority. There is much anger about the NY train crash, much press questions asking why there was not automated safety systems, yet a year ago had someone brought up the question of extra money to outfit the  trains with automated control systems there would have been outrage.

I just started Bruce Schneierâ€™s latest book â€œ[Carry On: Sound Advice from Schneier on Security](http://www.amazon.com/Carry-Sound-Advice-Schneier-Security-ebook/dp/B00GTQBIF0/)â€. In the first chapter he covers the paradox of how humans when faced with a loss situation will choose large loss with high-risk over a sure small risk. This is referred to as the [Prospect Theory](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospect_theory). It is not the typical logical approach, but does seem to be proven out as a human approach. It shows why it is hard to use a 'security' feature as a reason to buy one product over another. It is also why it is hard to use a 'safety' feature as a reason. Too often this means fear is used, which is an equally noneffective approach.

[mHealth developers](http://healthcaresecprivacy.blogspot.com/2013/03/privacy-and-security-in-designing.html) should be allowed to develop applications, but they should not be allowed to cause patient harm through negligence. This is all that FDA regulates. One can follow FDA with little overhead. The alternative is to wait for the FDA to force a visible issue like 23andMe; or for a train wreck to happen for which blood is on your hands.
